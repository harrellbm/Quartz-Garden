After all our current generations is not the first nor will it be the last to witness the advent of technologies that deeply reshaped to workings of the world.

# Technology as a Narrative Tool
Technology is not value neutral. Every piece of technology works to realize a particular picture of human thriving and existence. In other words, no matter how primitive the tool (think of even a hammer) a piece of technology seeks to accomplish a particular kind of work. The goal being (theoretically) to make the life of the human using it better. A good example of this is the automobile. This piece of technology has vastly changed the ability of humans to personally inhabit a much larger area than would be possible on foot, or with other more ridged forms of transportation. 
# Technology to Spread the Gospel
We come from a long history of Christians who have harness new technology for the good and spread of the Gospel. Martin Luther famously put the then brand new printing press to great use during the reformation. Sharing booklets, sermons, and even the whole German Bible to larger numbers of people than ever had access to such resources before. Paul made extensive use of the technologically superior road and boat systems throughout the Roman empire. Without such technology, it is doubtful Paul would have been able to travel to as many cities and provinces during his missionary journeys. 

Yet while Luther and Paul made good uses of these technologies, this does not mean that they are therefore value neutral. Roman Emperors invested heavily in building roads, not for the sake of the Gospel, but so that they could quickly move armies around to maintain full and brutal control within the empire. Just because a piece of technology is effective at a job does not automatically mean that it is unequivocally good.
# Thresholds of Technology
One helpful way to sort through this dichotomy is the idea of "thresholds." Every technology has a threshold after which it no longer serve as an aid, but become a liability and even an addiction.  For example, Luther made strong use of the printing press for good, but Hitler and Stalin both had extensive printing and publicity programs within their regimes.  Paul traveled far and wide on roman roads, but the romans themselves used these same roads to bring crushing taxes and physical oppression to huge populations of people.[^1]
