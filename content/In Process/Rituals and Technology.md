# Introduction: What is in a Ritual?
From a religious perspective rituals are a sort of bridge into another realm or a connection point between the individual and God.

Yet no matter how far you may feel from organized religion or spirituality, you have some form of ritual in your life. All of us have those things which we do over and over because something in us values it. Rituals can be thought of as a snapshot of our repeated valuing of something. 

Similarly, Mantras, Liturgy, Routines, Disciplines, Plans, Goals, Schedules are all structures from which we attempt to organize our life around the activities we hope to be the most beneficial and impactful. 
Therefore, choosing the word ritual to describe this kind of human habit keeps in focus the deeper formative nature of our regular patterns of living.

# Basic Nature of a Ritual
A strong ritual acts as a behavioral loop. The more the loop runs the more the pattern of behavior ingrains itself within the individual. But this is not a static looping like a spinning tire. Rather, inhabiting the space of a ritual shapes us overtime. Like weightlifting, a single repetition will not accomplish much but the consistent repeated work is what builds strength. 

This does not always go as smoothly as intended, like the new years resolution that quickly falls apart, or the business plan that can't keep up with the realities of the industry. 

Yet every human craves a certain amount of repeated structure. A shape of life as expressed in a set of repeated rituals. This is exactly what all religious rituals set out to accomplish. They chart a path from who I am to who I want to be. 

However, not all rituals are purely intentionally initiated. Some are biologically driven (eating, sleeping). Others created and imposed by outside forces (paying taxes). 

Regardless of their impetus, all of these rituals orient us around particular things. They teach us what to value and what to discard. They shape us into particular kinds of people that we would not be without them. 

A classic natural metaphor that applies to rituals nicely is that a canyon is not carved by one drop of water but in the consistent flow of many drops. 
# Technological Rituals
Every technological tool has a human interface. A handle, keyboard, screen, lever, seat, etc. These interfaces are all sites of many different kinds of rituals. 

Because after all, in order to use any piece of technology there is a certain amount of repeated action. Or, as stated above, a repeated valuing of something. If nothing was being valued, then the tool would fall useless to the side. 

This means that as we step into further considerations of rituals and their connection with technology, we can begin by identifying the "seat of value." This core value is what makes a piece of technology worthwhile. It is also the engine that runs the behavior loops of ritual connected to the technology.
